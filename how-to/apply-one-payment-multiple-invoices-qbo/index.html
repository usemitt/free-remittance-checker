How to apply one payment to multiple invoices in QuickBooks Online

The problem

A customer pays several of your invoices with a single check, ACH transfer or wire. Often they also send a remittance advice listing which invoices the money covers; sometimes it's a handful, sometimes 50 or 100 lines.

QuickBooks Online (QBO) can apply one payment across many invoices for the same customer. The catch is that you select each invoice yourself in the Receive payment screen. There's no built-in option to upload the remittance file and have QBO tick the invoices for you. With a long remittance, the work is mostly finding the right invoices, checking amounts, and making sure the total lands exactly.

This guide covers the manual method step by step, the two common bank-deposit setups, how to handle short payments, and how to check a remittance before you start.

Step by step: record one payment against several invoices

Menu names below were accurate when written. Verify them against your current QBO screen, since labels and layouts change.

  1. Open Receive payment. Select + New (or + Create), then Receive payment. You can also open one of the customer's invoices and choose Receive payment from there.

  2. Choose the customer. Pick them from the Customer dropdown. QBO lists their open invoices under Outstanding Transactions.

  3. Enter the payment details. Set the Payment date, the Payment method (check, ACH, etc.) and the Reference no. (check number or the remittance reference).

  4. Choose where the money goes. In Deposit to:

    • choose the bank account if this payment shows up on its own on your bank statement; or
    • choose Undeposited Funds if it will be grouped with other payments into one bank deposit (see setup B below). Some newer QBO versions may label this differently, so verify in your UI.

    If you process payments through a merchant account, this field may not appear.

  5. Enter the Amount received. Type the total the customer paid.

  6. Select the invoices. Tick the checkbox next to each invoice the payment covers. When you select several, QBO fills in each invoice's open balance in the Payment column.

  7. Fix partial payments. If the customer paid less than the full amount on an invoice, overwrite that invoice's amount in the Payment column. The invoice stays open for the balance.

  8. Check the totals before saving. The amount to apply should equal the amount received. If you've received more than you've applied, QBO keeps the difference as a credit on the customer's account, which usually means a line was missed.

  9. Attach the remittance (optional but recommended). Add the remittance file as an attachment and note its reference in the memo, so you can trace the payment later.

  10. Save (Save and close).

Matching it to the bank feed

How the money arrived decides the last step.

Setup A: the payment is its own line on the bank statement. Record the payment with Deposit to set to the bank account, as above. In Banking (or Transactions → Bank transactions; verify in your UI), the downloaded deposit should show a suggested match to that payment. Review it and confirm the match.

Setup B: one bank deposit combines several payments (several checks deposited together, or a deposit covering more than one customer).

  1. Record each customer's payment with Receive payment, with Deposit to = Undeposited Funds.
  2. Select + New → Bank deposit, choose the bank account, tick all the payments that make up the deposit, and save.
  3. In Banking, match the downloaded deposit to that bank deposit.

Common snags

  • Invoice still shows open after the customer paid. Open the payment and check the right invoice is ticked. A payment saved without ticking invoices stays unapplied. To fix it, open Receive payment for that customer, tick the unapplied payment under Credits and the invoice under Outstanding Transactions, then save.
  • Credits applied to invoices you didn't expect. QBO has a setting that automatically applies a customer's existing credits to their oldest open invoices. You can check it under Settings → Account and settings → Advanced → Automation → Automatically apply credits (verify in your UI).
  • Two invoices with the same amount. Don't match on amount alone; use the invoice numbers from the remittance.
  • Customer's references don't look like your invoice numbers. Customers often add prefixes, drop leading zeros, or quote their own PO number. Search for the core number, and keep a note of how each customer formats references.

Before you start: check a long remittance

With a long remittance, most mistakes happen before you open QBO. A five-minute check up front saves hunting for a missing line later.

  1. Put the remittance into a spreadsheet with two columns: invoice number and amount paid.
  2. Add a total and confirm it equals the payment you received.
  3. Compare each line with your open invoices, for example by exporting an open-invoices or A/R aging report from QBO. Flag:
    • references that don't match any open invoice;
    • amounts that differ from the open balance (short- or over-payments);
    • duplicate lines.
  4. Sort the sheet the same way you'll work through Outstanding Transactions. Then tick invoices in that order, adjusting the flagged lines as you go.
