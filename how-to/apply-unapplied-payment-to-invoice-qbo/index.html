How to apply an unapplied payment to an invoice in QuickBooks Online

The problem

A customer paid you, the money is in QuickBooks Online (QBO), but their invoice still shows as open. Or you run a cash-basis Profit and Loss and see a line called Unapplied Cash Payment Income. Either way, you've got a payment that isn't linked to the invoice it was meant to pay.

The fix usually happens in one screen: link the payment to the right invoice in the Receive payment screen. This guide covers what "unapplied" means, how it happens, how to find unapplied payments, and how to apply them step by step.

What "unapplied payment" means

An unapplied payment is a customer payment recorded in QBO without being linked to an invoice (or other sales form). QBO treats the money as a credit sitting on the customer's account. Their balance goes down, but the invoice itself stays open.

If you report on a cash basis, you may also see two accounts QBO creates for this: Unapplied Cash Payment Income and Unapplied Cash Bill Payment Expense. Intuit says these are system accounts for cash-basis reporting that can't be deleted or changed, and you don't post to them directly. Unapplied Cash Payment Income shows payments received but not yet applied to a sales form.

How it happens

Intuit lists two main causes for Unapplied Cash Payment Income:

  • The customer prepaid, and you recorded the payment before creating the invoice.
  • A payment was entered without matching it to a sales form, for example Receive payment was saved without ticking any invoices.

Other common ways to end up with money sitting unapplied:

  • Overpayment. The amount received was more than the invoices ticked, so QBO kept the difference as a credit.
  • A remittance didn't line up. A customer paid many invoices in one payment, a few lines on the remittance didn't match your invoice numbers, and those amounts were left unapplied to sort out later. (See turning a remittance advice into a Receive payment.)
  • Recorded as a deposit. A payment recorded as a bank deposit with the customer's name and Accounts Receivable as the account also shows up as a credit you can apply.

Find unapplied payments

Menu names below were accurate when written. Verify them against your current QBO screen, since labels and layouts change.

Intuit's suggested route is the Open Invoices report:

  1. Go to Reports, then Standard reports.
  2. Search for Open Invoices and run it for the period you need.
  3. In the Transaction type column, look for any line listed as Payment. Those are payments not fully applied to invoices.
  4. Check whether each one has a matching open invoice on the same report.

You can also open the customer's record and look through their transaction list for payments that show an open or unapplied amount.

Step by step: apply the payment to an invoice

If the matching invoice already exists:

  1. From the Open Invoices report, select the Payment's date to open it. (Or open the invoice and choose Receive payment.)
  2. Under Outstanding Transactions, tick the invoice the payment was meant for.
  3. If you opened Receive payment fresh rather than the original payment, tick the unapplied payment under Credits as well.
  4. Check the totals. The amount applied should equal what you're using from the credit; adjust the Payment column if only part of it should go to this invoice.
  5. Select Save and close.
  6. Re-run Open Invoices and repeat for any remaining Payment lines.

If there's no invoice yet (for example, a prepayment):

  1. Create the invoice with the same customer, and the amount and date that fit the sale.
  2. Go back to the unapplied payment, open it, and tick the new invoice under Outstanding Transactions.
  3. Select Save and close.

Intuit's help suggests creating the invoice with the same customer, amount and date as the payment. It also notes that if the Unapplied Cash Payment Income amount looks wrong, the payment needs applying to an invoice dated before the payment. When in doubt about dating, ask your accountant.

Afterwards, run your cash-basis Profit and Loss again. Once all unapplied payments are linked, the Unapplied Cash Payment Income line should no longer appear.

Things to check before you link

  • Closed periods. If the payment or invoice falls in a year or period that's already closed, linking them can change reports for that period, even if you date the link later. In a QuickBooks Community thread, Intuit's team confirmed this and recommended consulting your accountant first.
  • Automatically apply credits. QBO has a setting that applies existing customer credits automatically. Intuit's help says that, by default, a credit or overpayment on file is applied to the customer's oldest open invoices. If that's not what you want, check Settings → Account and settings → Advanced → Automation → Automatically apply credits (verify in your UI). Even with it off, QBO may pre-check available credits on the Receive payment screen as a suggestion, so look before you save.
  • Applied to the wrong invoice. Open the payment, untick the wrong invoice, tick the right one, and save.
  • Credits stay with one customer. Intuit notes a customer's credit can only be applied to that same customer's transactions. If a payment was recorded under the wrong customer, fix the payment's customer rather than looking for a way to move the credit.
  • Bank feed. Linking a payment to an invoice doesn't match it to your bank feed. If the deposit is still waiting in Banking, match it there as well.

When a remittance is the cause

If your unapplied payments mostly come from customers who pay lots of invoices at once, the root cause is usually the remittance: references that don't match your invoice numbers, amounts that differ, or lines that got skipped.
